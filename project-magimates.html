Magimates

A little character.
A whole new way to charge.

Project type
Product & Brand Design
Stage
Launched Venture · Began 2024
Deliverables
Product, Packaging & Launch Visuals

The idea

Make the everyday
a little more joyful.

Magimates turns a MagSafe charging puck into a small companion for your desk or bedside table. What began as a bear-shaped stand grew into a family of characters—and a business I built with cofounder Zach Teiger.

I carried the idea from 3D models and physical prototypes through the identity, packaging, and imagery customers would see at launch.

My contribution

Product design, prototyping, packaging, branding, website visualization, and all still imagery.

Built together

Zach Teiger led operations and business development. We shared the creation of marketing videos.

01 / Product design

A character built
around a constraint.

The circular charging puck became the face. The rest of the form had to hold a phone at a useful angle, guide the cable through the body, and still read as a friendly animal.

Model → Make → Refine

Fit first.
Personality throughout.

The early work explored both 3D printing and resin molds. Printing became the production direction because it offered lower costs and more consistent results for the small batches we were making.

Physical iterations brought the design back to the details: seating the puck, feeding the cable, and removing the charger without losing the character’s simple silhouette.

One interface.
Four personalities.

The launch family used two animal forms and two finishes. Each kept the same charging interaction while giving customers a character to choose.

Product visualizations of the launch family. The stand houses a separate MagSafe charger.

02 / Brand identity

From an object
to a recognizable mate.

“Find your mate. Find your joy.” gave the brand a voice as playful as the product. I developed the identity and still imagery to make the characters feel familiar across a screen, a package, and a desk.

Find your mate. Find your joy.

A face you remember.

The smiling bear mark carries the product’s rounded forms into a simple graphic. Character names, soft color fields, and close-up product views gave the family a consistent visual language.

That language could stretch from clear catalog imagery to more expressive campaign compositions.

Brand and social visualizations by Jared Teiger.

03 / Packaging & setup

The first meeting
starts inside the box.

I designed the packaging as an extension of the character: a clear outer identity, an insert shaped around the product, and illustrated guidance for the first setup.

Test the structure.
Then build the experience.

Hand-cut cardboard trials tested how the product sat inside the box and how it could be lifted out. Those trials connected the packaging graphics to the practical work of making, packing, and sending a physical product.

After the unboxing

Make the first
interaction clear.

The instruction card breaks inserting and releasing the MagSafe puck into two short sequences. The same physical details developed in the product—cable routing, fit, and access—become the information the customer needs first.

04 / Marketing & launch

Give the product
a world to live in.

The launch needed imagery that could explain the function and express the brand. I created the still visuals for the website and marketing, while Zach and I worked together on video content.

From product view
to everyday use.

Clean renders made the characters easy to compare. Photography showed how the stand held a phone in a real setting. Together, they gave the storefront both a clear product story and a sense of personality.

Video work extended that story through setup demonstrations, unboxing, and character-led content.

Exploring a larger presence.

Campaign mockups tested how the identity could work at a larger scale: simple messages, generous product views, and color that gives each character room to stand out.

Building the venture

Beyond a finished design.

A product becomes a business when the design has to work repeatedly—for making, presenting, packing, and selling.

With Zach leading operations and business development, I connected the product and visual identity across the customer experience. Magimates launched through its own website and Etsy, established a social presence, and made its first sales.

Small-batch production also made the constraints tangible. Printing offered flexibility, but production time became a capacity challenge. The next question was how to keep the product consistent beyond the first batches.

01

Design for the whole journey.

The product, setup card, package, and storefront all need to explain the same idea.

02

Make production part of design.

Material choices and print time affect what a small business can reliably deliver.

03

Build a family, not a single image.

A consistent interface and visual language let new characters feel like they belong.
