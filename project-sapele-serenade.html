Introduction

Sapele Serenade brings together a sculpted Sapele hardwood frame and a hand-woven seat and backrest. Designed for lounging, the chair pairs the warmth of wood with an open, tactile surface—developed through sketches, physical models, and hands-on making.

The Brief

A chair to build, sit in, and live with.

The design brief called for a full-scale working chair with considered ergonomics and no upholstery. The challenge was to develop the seat, back, and structure together, then carry the design through to a finished physical object.

My mood studies drew on Spanish architecture, natural sunlight, and the muted tones of arid environments. Woven lounge seating offered a way to connect those references to a material and a making process.

The Vision

Bring the warmth of a crafted wood frame and the texture of hand weaving into one inviting lounge chair.

Finding the Form

I began with a brutalist direction: sharp angles, sheet metal, and wood. As the project developed, I shifted toward a warmer wood frame with woven surfaces.

The next sketches explored how the backrest, seat, and legs could read as a continuous profile. Openings beneath the seat gave each concept a different balance between solid structure and negative space.

Concept Development

Move from a drawn silhouette to a form that can be seen from every angle.

Physical scale models let me compare the frame profiles beyond the side view. The woven seat and back became part of those studies, so the surface and structure developed together.

Across the concepts, the central question stayed the same: how could the frame support a relaxed seating posture while keeping a clear, expressive silhouette?

From Model to Full Scale

Resolve the proportions in CAD, then bring them into the room.

I worked between dimensioned CAD studies and a rough full-scale model to develop the seat, backrest, and frame proportions. The physical build brought the design out of the screen and into human scale.

With the overall direction established, the work moved toward refining the frame and preparing it for construction in Sapele hardwood.

Developing the Surface

The weave is part of the design.

Small woven samples explored how changes in the over-and-under pattern affected the surface. Those studies continued into larger samples and the chair models.

In the finished chair, blue webbing contrasts with the reddish-brown Sapele. The repeated pattern gives the seat and backrest a shared rhythm while leaving the wood frame visible.

Fabrication

From shaped hardwood to a complete frame, one connection and surface at a time.

The workshop sequence followed the design from hardwood components through shaping, joinery, and assembly. The side frames established the silhouette; crossbars connected them into a complete chair.

After assembly, the surfaces were refined and finished before the woven seat and back were added. This stage translated the clean lines of the models into the details of a handmade object.

Weaving the Chair

An open frame becomes a place to sit.

The webbing wraps around the frame and interlaces across the seat and back. The in-progress photographs show how individual strips gradually become a continuous surface.

Wood and weave meet along the edges, making the construction visible in the finished chair.

Sapele Serenade

Warm hardwood. A woven surface. A form made for slowing down.

The finished chair brings the project’s two material ideas together: a continuous wood profile and a patterned woven surface. Its character comes from the relationship between those elements and the handwork that connects them.
